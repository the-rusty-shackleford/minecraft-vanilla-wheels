# D-0002 — The driver's client drives; climbing is vanilla's step; rolling resistance

**Decision**: A vehicle with a player at the wheel is moved by that player's client
(`isControlledByLocalInstance`, the boat rule); the server re-runs the claimed move with
the same `Drive.step` and `move` and resets a client that disagrees by more than a
quarter block; a driverless vehicle is server-simulated. `maxUpStep()` returns the
profile's `climb`, so vanilla's `collide` puts the box on top of any ledge that high in
the tick it meets it, identically on both sides, and `Suspension` eases the drawn body
and the riders up over a few ticks. Off the throttle, `Drive` takes a fixed rolling
resistance per tick on top of drag.

**Why**: Driving has to feel immediate, and the server-validated client move is how the
game already does it for boats. Any climbing trick that lives only on the client would
be reset by the server's re-simulation, so climbing must be the collision code's own
step; the snap that produces is a rendering concern, hidden by the suspension. Drag
alone (one percent a tick) left a car rolling for a quarter of a minute -- the gametest
caught it -- while a constant deceleration gives engine braking that stops a pickup in
under five seconds without touching the throttled top speed.

**Alternatives rejected**: *Server-driven with client prediction* -- lag between key and
motion, and a second copy of the model. *A jump on a ledge* -- awkward, and an ask of
the spec was that it not be. *Stronger drag* -- it also lowers the steady speed under
throttle, coupling two feelings that should tune separately.
