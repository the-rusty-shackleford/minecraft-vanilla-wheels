# D-0003 — The mesh renderer: our OBJ parser, normals from geometry, paint as vertex colour

**Decision**: Meshes are Wavefront OBJ files read by a JDK-only parser into an immutable
`Mesh`; normals are computed by Newell's method and oriented away from each connected
convex piece's centroid, never taken from winding; n-gons are fanned into quads; the
client bakes each profile's mesh once per reload into float arrays and draws the body
through one `entityCutoutNoCull` buffer with the dye as the vertex colour on paint
parts, lamp parts fullbright when lit, needles rotated about their pivots, wheels
translated and spun per slot, and glass last through the translucent type. A bad OBJ
logs its line and renders a placeholder box.

**Why**: The bundle's frame has 98 of 764 faces wound backward and no normals, yet every
connected piece is convex, so the centroid rule is the only one that gives a correct
outward normal for every face. A parser of our own keeps the domain layer free of the
modding classpath, so the whole mesh library is unit-tested against the real bundle as a
fixture. The fragment shader multiplies texture by vertex colour, so grey swatches under
a dye are paint for free and one texture serves every colour. No back-face culling
because the cabin is seen from inside.

**Alternatives rejected**: *A JSON block model* -- a pickup is 764 faces with rotated
dials and n-gons; the model format is cubes. *Trusting winding* -- wrong on an eighth of
the faces. *One texture per dye colour* -- sixteen textures per vehicle.
