# D-0004 — The Mechanic Lift: the assembly is the profile; a 5 x 7 deck; the index finds the controller

**Decision**: What builds a vehicle on the lift is derived from its profile -- a chassis,
one wheel per wheel position, an engine iff the profile has one (`domain.Assembly`) --
not declared by a recipe type. The lift is a deck five cells wide and seven long with
two-block corner posts, forty-three blocks: a controller (the only block entity, at the
front centre) and forty-two parts whose block state carries the lift's facing and the
cell's index, so any part finds its controller by arithmetic (`domain.Footprint`) with
no level lookup. Placement validates the whole footprint in `getStateForPlacement`, so
the item places nothing when refused, and lays the parts in `setPlacedBy`. Removal of
any cell removes the controller, whose removal removes the rest; the drop happens once,
in the hook that knows a player broke it. The menu's verdicts are computed on the server
each tick and sent down data slots; the client's menu holds no position at all and is
opened by the vanilla screen packet.

**Why**: The plan carried an `AssemblyRecipe` type, but every recipe it could express
restated the profile (the wheel count is the wheel positions; the engine is the engine),
and a separate file invites two recipes for one chassis, which the plan then had to
refuse. Deriving the rule removes a serializer, a recipe type, JSON, and that failure
mode. Five wide rather than the plan's four puts the controller at a true centre and
gives a 2.75-block pickup a block of margin each side. The index in the state is what
makes every hook -- remove, destroy, click, shape -- answer from the state it is handed.
The vanilla open-screen packet, rather than NeoForge's with a position, is what lets a
mock player with a dead connection open the menu in a gametest, and the client never
needed the position.

**Alternatives rejected**: *A recipe type* -- above. *A block entity per part* -- forty-two
tick-less entities per lift for what an integer property does. *A one-block lift* -- a
block's collision shape cannot exceed its block, so a vehicle could not stand on it.
