# D-0006 — A vehicle is authored in Blockbench: the `.bbmodel` is read as it is; the dye is lifted; the glass clears for the rider

**Decision**: The protocol reads a Blockbench project file (`.bbmodel`, the free or Java
Block format) directly: its cubes with their rotations, its mesh elements, the outliner's
folders as the faces' groups, and the texture embedded in it, with `texture` in the
profile optional. OBJ stays (D-0003), but a vehicle is meant to be made and looked at in
Blockbench, and the file Blockbench saves is the file the game loads. Selectors name
folders and elements, never materials, since a one-texture project has one material.

Paint is the dye's colour lifted a quarter of the way toward white before it multiplies
into the body's grey swatches (`domain.Paint`). The glass part is drawn at a third of its
alpha for whoever is aboard, so it can read as glass from outside and be clear from the
driver's seat.

**Rationale**: The first Trailblazer was generated as an OBJ by a script and judged by its
numbers; Rusty saw a windshield that did not meet its frame, a dash a driver could not see
past, and a cab that was a brick, none of which a measurement caught. Blockbench shows the
thing. Authoring there, and reviewing renders of the project before any booth run, is the
only loop that surfaces those defects; reading the project as saved means a hand edit in
Blockbench ships without an export step that could lose it. The lift exists because
light-blue dye times any grey is teal: a multiply can only darken, and the reference's sky
blue has more red in it than the dye. A quarter keeps red red and turns black into a dark
grey, which is what a black car is. The rider's fade is the smallest change that gives
both looks; a pane that is clear for everyone loses the glass, and one that is opaque for
the driver loses the road.

**Consequences**: A `.bbmodel` face's UV rows run from the top, so the parser flips them
to the mesh's bottom-up convention; this was found by a truck that drew nothing, since
every face sampled the atlas's empty half and the cutout shader dropped it. Rotations are
Blockbench's: about +X, a positive angle carries the top toward +Z, the nose, so a
windshield's rake is negative; found by a frame that leaned forward like a bus. A
vehicle's generator writes the project for Blockbench to open, plus a preview project
with the wheels in place and the paint tinted, and the review is of renders of that
preview taken through Blockbench's own camera, not of the numbers.
