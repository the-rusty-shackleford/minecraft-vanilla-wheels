# D-0008 — nfx's patch layer ported into source: the terrain pose, the tow order, the gestures

**Decision**: nfx's two days of driving-feel work, shipped to his instance as ASM splices
over the 1.4.0 jar (`vanillawheels-1.4.0.33`), are source here and his patch layer
retires. Ported as designed: `domain/Terrain` (his `GroundPose`/`TrailerPose`: disc-contact
wheel probes, a least-squares plane over the footprint with the wall walk, critically
damped tilt springs, height on the plane within sink and float bounds, the descending-end
clearance, the towed body as a lever on its axle) behind a `Columns` sampler so it runs
under JUnit with synthetic ground, with the pose memory on the entity; the seat anchored
on the rider's eye (`R(eye) - e`) and the rider drawn turned about the eye, animals about
their feet; turn-in-place in `Drive.step`; a towed body catching up at the top of its own
tick when its tower ticked first, on a pass clock (`TickClock`), else moved from the
tower's tick, its old pose restored if the snapshot clobbered it; lights up the tow chain;
catch and stretch in the ground plane; a trailer placed hitched by clicking a car with the
item; the door click only toggling and a lead at the open door unloading; seatless
vehicles' hit boxes solid to walkers; marker lamps for unpowered vehicles. Kept from
D-0007 over his: the server's re-run of a reported move, stood on the ground first, rather
than his verbatim `setPos`; the camera distance by the vehicle's length. Two things his
design lacked, added: a ceiling is never ground (a solid run with air under it is skipped),
found when the box car's roofed arena lifted the body to the ceiling; and a vehicle on the
Mechanic Lift rises with the deck. `climb` is 1.0 for the pickup, his finding that
two-block climbs lurch worst.

**Why**: Rusty chose his for the driver's view (full-size truck, headrests gone, a level
camera) and for Trailer 2, and asked for the port. His pose is plainly better than the
per-axle chase it replaces: one angle up a staircase, no nod per riser, no rear against a
wall. The server-side replay choice is the one place the two designs differ in what the
server trusts, and keeping the server's own collision is the more stable of the two under
a bad connection (D-0007 tells the stall's cause; his verbatim apply can never refuse but
also never stops a desynced client pushing a truck through a wall).

**Alternatives rejected**: *Keeping the patch layer* -- a splicer over bytecode nobody can
read is a maintenance debt the moment either side changes. *His pass clock as static
weak maps per side* -- the state belongs on the entity; the clock alone stays static.

**Addendum, the same day**: three of his open items closed. Footprint collision: the nose's
and tail's corners and centres are checked at a move's destination against blocks that span
the climb line, and the move is cut short at the wall (`Vehicle.footprintClamp`), a wall
now stalling the drive (`Drive.halted`); the terrain samples look as deep as the fit's window
is long (`Shape.sampleBelow`), which took the ripple off a long body's descent; a door's
painted panels take the dye (`Appearance.Hinge.painted`). The reported first-person flip
did not reproduce: the playtest's first-person run logs the pose every tick and its pitch
stayed within twelve degrees. And the pose is synced after all (Rusty's call): the
simulating side shares lift, pitch and roll as they change (`Vehicle.sharePose`, the
`Pose` payload for a client, the synced data for all), and every other side draws and seats
with them; the server never takes a player's wheel, so it never fights the driver's pose.

And storage became chests (Rusty): a list, each the game's double chest drawn at its own
place and scale with its own rows and lid, opened by right-clicking the chest itself -- a
chest a click, the rest of the body a seat -- the inventories end to end in the vehicle's
one item list.

**Addendum, 2026-09-13, after the release**: three of Rusty's reports off pack 1.31.0. The
third-person camera stuttered on their meadows: the re-placement through leaves clipped on
a block's outline shape, so every grass tuft a slope put behind the truck pulled the camera
in; it clips on the visual shape now, the game's own rule for its camera (the booth looks
up from the seat across a meadow and reads the distance; the old code fails it at 3.8 of
6). The Trailblazer's chest could not be opened from outside: the click's hit point lies on
the hull's box, over a chest sunk in the bed; the click's line is followed on from the
hit (`domain/RayBox`). The truck stuck on hillsides: the footprint's wall rule measured
every point against the body's own height, so a nose over the second riser of a staircase
saw a wall before the box had climbed the first; each point is reached by a walk from the
centre following the ground, and a riser is a wall only if it is taller than the climb over
the ground before it. With it, the clamp slides along a wall met at a slant (each axis
tried alone) and the stall is proportional to the ground the world refused
(`Drive.slowed`), so a corner's graze costs a little instead of everything.

**Addendum, 2026-09-14, the spill flake's second cause**: hunted by hooking every item's
removal and pickup on the server with a stack trace (kept in the gametest mod) and looping
the gametest server in the background until it failed: a mock rider, made at the world's
origin, sweeps for items on its first tick aboard over the box round itself and its vehicle
together -- the game's rule for a riding player -- and that box reached from the origin to
its car, over half the test grid. Mock riders now stand at the car first. The lesson for the
next flake: instrument the taker, don't loop blind.

**Addendum, 2026-09-14, the road's texture**: Rusty: "the driving is a little tweaky ... it
randomly stops handling turn input"; a rugged lane in the playtest (a diagonal hillside, a
jagged descent, a bump field, ridges at forty-five degrees, a stair a riser every block,
moguls) with the wheel weaving showed the cause: at speed over rough ground the box is
airborne a few ticks at a time, the drive ignores the wheel in the air, and the game steps
nothing up in the air, so a riser met an inch off the ground was a wall (two to four stalls
per run, a third of the weave's steering ticks with no turn). Now a body within its climb
of the ground is grounded for the wheel and the step (`Vehicle.grounded`), and the server's
re-run of a reported step keeps the driver's ground flag (the game flags a body that
stepped as on the ground even hovering over the riser's edge; the re-run's upward move
left its copy airborne there, and 43 rejections a run followed). After: no stall, top speed
held through every section, no turn lost but the wheel's own pass through centre. Rusty's
two-block cheat was not needed and the two-block wall stays a wall.
