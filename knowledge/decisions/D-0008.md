# D-0008 — nfx's patch layer ported into source: the terrain pose, the tow order, the gestures

**Decision**: nfx's two days of driving-feel work, shipped to his instance as ASM splices
over the 1.4.0 jar (`vanillawheels-1.4.0.33`), are source here and his patch layer
retires. Ported as designed: `domain/Terrain` (his `GroundPose`/`TrailerPose`: disc-contact
wheel probes, a least-squares plane over the footprint with the wall walk, critically
damped tilt springs, height on the plane within sink and float bounds, the descending-end
clearance, the towed body as a lever on its axle) behind a `Columns` sampler so it runs
under JUnit with synthetic ground, with the pose memory on the entity; the seat anchored
on the rider's eye (`R(eye) - e`) and the rider drawn turned about the eye, animals about
their feet; turn-in-place in `Drive.step`; a towed body catching up at the top of its own
tick when its tower ticked first, on a pass clock (`TickClock`), else moved from the
tower's tick, its old pose restored if the snapshot clobbered it; lights up the tow chain;
catch and stretch in the ground plane; a trailer placed hitched by clicking a car with the
item; the door click only toggling and a lead at the open door unloading; seatless
vehicles' hit boxes solid to walkers; marker lamps for unpowered vehicles. Kept from
D-0007 over his: the server's re-run of a reported move, stood on the ground first, rather
than his verbatim `setPos`; the camera distance by the vehicle's length. Two things his
design lacked, added: a ceiling is never ground (a solid run with air under it is skipped),
found when the box car's roofed arena lifted the body to the ceiling; and a vehicle on the
Mechanic Lift rises with the deck. `climb` is 1.0 for the pickup, his finding that
two-block climbs lurch worst.

**Why**: Rusty chose his for the driver's view (full-size truck, headrests gone, a level
camera) and for Trailer 2, and asked for the port. His pose is plainly better than the
per-axle chase it replaces: one angle up a staircase, no nod per riser, no rear against a
wall. The server-side replay choice is the one place the two designs differ in what the
server trusts, and keeping the server's own collision is the more stable of the two under
a bad connection (D-0007 tells the stall's cause; his verbatim apply can never refuse but
also never stops a desynced client pushing a truck through a wall).

**Alternatives rejected**: *Keeping the patch layer* -- a splicer over bytecode nobody can
read is a maintenance debt the moment either side changes. *His pass clock as static
weak maps per side* -- the state belongs on the entity; the clock alone stays static.
