# D-0023 — Every vehicle's own crowbar

Rusty, 2026-09-28: "crafting any vehicle should also create a crowbar that goes in its inventory.
If that specific crowbar ever gets lost or dropped for any reason, it should return to a special
slot in the vehicle inventory. Basically, a crowbar should only ever exist in a vehicle's
inventory or on a person or their inventory (including any backpacks worn). Spare crowbars may be
crafted and have no such restrictions, but every vehicle also gets its own crowbar." Then his
choices: a one-slot toolbox screen; prying with its own crowbar packs it inside; vehicles already
in the world get one at first load.

- **Identity.** A vehicle has its own id (`VehicleId` on the entity, `vanillawheels:vehicle_id` on
  the packed item). It is kept through packing, placing, recall and wrecks. Its crowbar carries
  `vanillawheels:crowbar_of` (the id and the profile, which names it in the tooltip). A spare
  crowbar carries neither.
- **Birth.** A vehicle entering a server level without an id gets one and a new crowbar in its
  slot (`onAddedToLevel`). That covers the lift's build, a catalogue item placed, and every
  vehicle saved before 1.10.0.
- **The toolbox.** A crouching, empty-handed click anywhere on the body except a door, the tongue
  or the radio opens a one-slot screen (`ToolboxMenu`) over the player's inventory. It is laid out
  as a hopper. The slot takes this vehicle's own crowbar and nothing else, and an empty slot shows
  the crowbar's outline and says what goes there. It is a plain menu: the vehicle's id rides its
  four data slots, because a menu opened with extra data needs a NeoForge payload the gametests'
  mock players cannot receive. An open toolbox holds the vehicle, as an open chest does.
- **Packing.** Pried with its own crowbar, the crowbar goes back into the slot and travels inside
  the packed item (`vanillawheels:own_crowbar`). Pried with any other crowbar, that crowbar stays
  in hand.
- **Going home** (`OwnCrowbars`). The crowbar may be in its slot, in the player's inventory, or in
  a bag Carried says the player carries (pocketed or worn). Anywhere else it goes home: into the
  slot if its vehicle is loaded, else into the world's record (`vanillawheels_own_crowbars`), and
  the vehicle claims it the next time it loads or is placed. It is caught:
  - as an item entity about to join a level (a toss, a death's spill), which is cancelled;
  - in a container that keeps things in the world (a block's, an entity's, a double chest), at
    once, as the menu's slots change, before a hopper can take it;
  - in any other container when its menu closes (an ender chest, a horse's pack). Passing slots,
    a crafting grid or an anvil, are handed back by the game first, so renaming it works;
  - when handed to an entity (an item frame, an armour stand, an allay, a villager's gift), which
    is refused;
  - when a block takes it on a right-click (a decorated pot, a display shelf): the block is looked
    into at the end of the tick, through its container or its item handler, and gives it up.
- **Limits.** The creative inventory's delete slot and `/clear` destroy it. Creative cloning can
  make two; a second one going home to a full slot is discarded. Automation that takes straight
  from a player's inventory is not watched (nothing in the pack does). The index of loaded
  vehicles is held in memory. It costs no per-tick work beyond a check of an empty list.

**The data components hold stacks through `HeldStack`.** The game requires a component to be an
immutable value with equals and hashCode, and its dev check refused a packed vehicle carrying a
bare `ItemStack`. The radio's `disc` component had the same flaw and had never been packed in a
test with a disc in. Both now go through `HeldStack`, which saves exactly as the bare stack did, so
packed vehicles from before read the same.

Protocol "6": a new menu type and new components; clients update with the server.
