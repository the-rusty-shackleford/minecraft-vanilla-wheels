# D-0026 — One key per vehicle, marked, never lost

Rusty, 2026-09-29, making a paired car pack only for its key's owner (D-0025): "Paired to key fob
(which we need a way to mark to each car for organization and to ensure does not ever get lost)".

- **One key per vehicle.** D-0014 kept one pairing per player: pairing a second vehicle silently
  unpaired the first. `RecoveryData` now keys its entries by the vehicle's binding, with an index
  by key token, so a player pairs as many vehicles as they like and each key recalls its own. A
  blank key (or one whose pairing has moved on) pairs a vehicle; a blank at a vehicle the player
  already holds the key to issues a new key and retires the old, which becomes an ordinary item. A
  key at another vehicle is refused (it would strand its own vehicle's pairing), as is a vehicle
  another player holds the key to. Saves from before load unchanged: each old entry was one
  vehicle.
- **The mark, for organization.** A key is named for its vehicle (`item_name`: "Key to %s", the
  vehicle's custom name or its type) and banded in its paint: a tinted band on the fob's model
  (`key_colour`, tint 0; a blank's band is grey). The mark is kept in the pairing too, so a
  replacement can wear it with the vehicle far away. Clicking the vehicle with its own key
  refreshes the mark after a rename or repaint; a recall refreshes it. Pairings saved before
  1.10.0 have no mark until then.
- **Never lost.** `KeyFobs`, grown from D-0023's crowbar guard with its home changed from the
  vehicle's toolbox to the key's owner. A live key may rest only in its owner's inventory or a bag
  Carried says they carry. Anywhere else it goes home: into the owner's inventory or bag at once
  when they are online, alive and have room, else into a saved waiting list delivered at their
  login, their respawn, or within a second of their making room. Caught: an item entity about to
  join the world (tossed, spilled; a key inside a dropped bag, shulker box or bundle is taken out
  and the bag lies there without it, nested four deep); every slot of every menu a player opens
  (a block's or an entity's container at once, others on close; in anyone's inventory but the
  owner's at once); a hand-over to an item frame, armour stand or allay (refused); a decorated pot
  (given up at the tick's end); and a death, where `LivingDeathEvent` takes every live key off the
  player through Carried before anything drops, so no key lies on the ground or goes into another
  mod's grave (with keepInventory nothing moves). Only a `/clear` or the creative bin can take one.
- **A replacement** is a blank used in the air: it becomes the first of the player's keys, in
  pairing order, that is neither on them nor waiting to come home, wearing that pairing's mark,
  and the lost one stops working. With every key on them it is refused and stays blank.

## Rejected

- **Keys may sit in chests** for organization. A chest can burn, be broken into lava or be looted;
  "never lost" means on the owner. Organization is the mark and a bag.
- **Home to the vehicle** (D-0023's crowbar went to its toolbox): a key is how a distant vehicle is
  recalled, so a key sent to a vehicle nobody can reach would be lost in effect.
- **A keyring item** holding every key: more than was asked; a bag holds keys already.

## Verified

Gametests `KeyFobGameTests` (5): two vehicles, two live keys, named and banded; a key at another
vehicle refused and unchanged; a repaint refreshed by its own key without retiring it; a
stranger's blank refused; each key recalls its own vehicle; a blank at a paired vehicle retires
the old key, which can then be dropped; a key tossed, put in a chest, put in a stranger's
inventory, and dropped inside a bag comes home (the bag lies there without it; with no room it
waits, and comes within a second of a slot freeing); an item frame refuses it; two keys at a
death wait and come back at the respawn; a key sent home to an owner who is away comes at their
login; a blank replaces the key that is gone, marked, and is refused while none is. The booth
photographs a paired key in hand.
