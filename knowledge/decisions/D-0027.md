# D-0027 — A vehicle drives through an open garage door

Rusty, 2026-09-29: "my buddy cannot drive his trailblazer through an open garage door. It seems
the garage door places some kind of invisible obstruction." (Bobandy_ was driving a Trailblazer on
the box that evening.)

## The cause

A block's two-argument `getCollisionShape(level, pos)` returns a shape cached once per block state
at startup, computed with an empty world, unless the block declares `dynamicShape()`
(`BlockBehaviour.BlockStateBase.initCache`, 1.21.1). The garage door's shape is its block entity's
travel, and with no block entity behind it the door answers with its fallback: a full-height
panel, 6/16 to 10/16, through the middle of the cell. So every reader of the cached shape saw
every door cell shut, open or not. The vehicle's own box collides through the three-argument call,
which is never cached, and saw the door open. The footprint that keeps a vehicle's nose and tail
out of walls (D-0007, `Vehicle.footprintBlockedAt`) reads the cached one. So the nose met a
phantom panel in the doorway on client and server alike, which is why nothing showed as "moved
wrongly" in the log. The terrain pose reads it too.

It was never size-specific. Driven at a fifth of a block a tick, the Trailblazer-sized truck
stopped with its nose at z 10.37 (the panel's face) at a 3×3 and a 4×4 door, and the 1.5-wide
box car stopped at the same face. The old checks missed it because none drove up to a door at a
driver's pace. `GarageDoorGameTests` read the block entity's `shape()` directly. The booth's "vehicle
moves through open garage without a hidden barrier" moved the box car six blocks in one call, so
the footprint was tested only at the far end, clear of the door.

## The fix

The block declares `dynamicShape()`. Nothing is cached for it, and every reader gets the block
entity's travel. No saved data, network protocol or behaviour of a closed door changes.

## Verified

`GarageDoorGameTests.aTruckDrivesThroughAnOpenDoorAtDrivingSpeed` (namespace
`vanillawheels_garage_drive`, in the default run). It places real panels for a 3×3 and a 4×4
door for `box_truck`, a gametest profile with the Trailblazer's body, parts, wheels, seats and
climb, and a 3-wide, 4-high door for the box car. It opens them with redstone and asserts that
every cell's cached-path shape equals its panel's shape. Then it drives each vehicle through at
0.2 blocks a tick and asserts it is past the door. Without `dynamicShape()`, the first version
stopped all three at the panel's face and the final version fails at "cell 0,0 reads its open
shape". With it, the test passes. The booth's drive-through now moves in thirty fifth-of-a-block
steps.

## Not fixed: a door's top row against a very high climb

*Superseded by [D-0028](D-0028.md) (1.10.1): the footprint reads a block's cross-section at the
climb line, and the box car goes through a 3x3 door.*

The footprint tests a block as a wall by its shape's `bounds()`. In the edge columns of a door's
top row, the housing and the side track are two boxes, and their bounds fill the whole row of the
cell. A vehicle whose climb line (climb + 0.05 over the ground) reaches the top row meets that as
a wall. That happens when a door is under climb + 2 blocks high. The box car's test climb of 2.0 stops
at a 3-high door that way. Every shipped vehicle has a climb of 1.0, so only a 2-high door is
affected, and its 1.5 blocks under the housing is lower than all of them (1.73, 2.79, 2.99): the
visible housing stops them first. Testing each box, or the column under the point, would fix it.
Either would also loosen the footprint at fences and stair flights for every vehicle, so it waits
for Rusty's call and is not part of this fix.

## Rejected

- **The footprint reads the three-argument shape with the vehicle's context**, as the box's
  collision does. It would mend vehicles only, while pathfinding and every other reader of the
  cache still saw a shut door. The door was what reported its shape wrongly.
