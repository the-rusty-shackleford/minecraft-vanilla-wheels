# D-0028 — A block is a wall where it stands across the climb line; the fit starts where the body stands

Rusty, 2026-09-29: "I want that second garage door wall bug fixed." That is the defect D-0027 left
open: a garage door's top row walls a vehicle whose climb line reaches it.

## The cause

The footprint (D-0007, `Vehicle.footprintBlockedAt`) read each block as one box, its shape's
`bounds()`. A shape of several boxes collapsed to their bounding box. In the edge cells of a door's
top row the housing (y 0.5 to 1, z 0.2 to 0.8) and the side track (x 0 to 0.1, y 0 to 1) bound the
whole row of the cell, so a climb line under the housing met a wall across the doorway at its
corners. Reproduced on 1.10.0 by making the box car's door in
`GarageDoorGameTests.aTruckDrivesThroughAnOpenDoorAtDrivingSpeed` 3x3 again. The car (climb 2.0)
stopped at z 8.70 with its nose at 10.20, the housing's face.

## The decision

A block is a wall at a footprint point when the point lies in the rectangle, in x and z, that
bounds the block's boxes spanning the climb line (`domain/CrossSection`). The ground followed
under the walk is still a block's full bounds, as the box that climbs onto it meets them.

Where every box of a block spans the line (a full block, a slab, a fence, a wall, a pane), the
rectangle is the bounds, so the check gives 1.10.0's answer number for number. Where some boxes
do not span it, the rectangle lies within the bounds. So with the ground unchanged, the new check
finds no wall that 1.10.0 did not. It only removes walls: a door's housing over the line no
longer walls the doorway, and a stair's front half is open when the line crosses its upper half.

## Why this one, and not a box or a column at the point

D-0027 named two fixes, testing each box and testing the column under the point, and warned
that both loosen the footprint at fences and stair flights. Both were measured.

- **A model of the check** (`devtools/footprint/compare_rules.py`, the walk, the clamp and the
  four rules on flat ground) reproduced the box car's stop at 10.20. Over 800 random approaches
  to fence and wall layouts, the cross-section rule stopped exactly where 1.10.0 did every
  time. Testing each box, or the column, stopped elsewhere in 30 of 400 fence runs and 148 of
  400 wall runs, more often deeper in the fence or wall. At a lone fence corner met at an angle,
  per-box ended at least 0.03 deeper than 1.10.0 in 353 of 15,000 approaches, at worst with
  the post wholly inside the truck where 1.10.0 kept it clear. A fence corner's boxes make an L,
  and its bounds add the empty quadrant. A nose point in that quadrant with the post between two
  points was stopped at the cell's face, and per-box let it on 0.375.
- **The stair hazard is real, but not where it was named.** A clean flight cannot make it,
  since stairs rise at most one block over any block of run and the walk samples at most a
  block apart. A flight whose top step is a full block can. From a stair's front half, half a
  block up, the walk's next sample reaches the landing 1.5 higher, a wall to a column-read
  ground, though the box climbs it a block at a time. At speed the truck steps over that false
  wall, about 0.27 deep, because the footprint checks where a move ends and not the way there. A
  truck creeping from a standstill at the foot of the stairs stops there for good.

`FootprintGameTests` (namespace `vanillawheels_footprint`) pins all of it with `box_truck`, the
Trailblazer's body and its climb of 1. The climb tests send two trucks each, one on the gas
and one creeping 0.05 a tick. Each stop is logged as a `FOOTPRINT` line. On 1.10.0 and on this
change every number is the same to four decimals:

| Test | 1.10.0 and D-0028 |
|---|---|
| fence line: nose at the post's face, 24.375 | 24.3746 |
| riser two high: nose at 24 | 23.9995 |
| fence corner, a point in the empty quadrant: nose at the cell's face, 24 | 23.9995 |
| wall, posts under torches, a point beside a post: nose at the post's face, 24.25 | 24.2496 |
| stairs up, stairs to a landing, half slabs up, under a lintel, at speed: x | 35.1837 |
| stairs down, at speed: x | 32.5562 |
| creeping: stairs up / to a landing / half slabs / lintel / stairs down: x | 21.83 / 20.83 / 24.83 / 24.83 / 21.83 |

Each test was run against a rule it should catch:

| Mutant | Fails |
|---|---|
| per-box walls | fence corner (24.15), wall posts (24.31) |
| per-column walls and ground | fence corner, wall posts, stairs to a landing (the creeping truck stuck at 15.68, nose on the landing's face) |
| no footprint | riser, fence line, fence corner, wall posts |
| anything over the line within the hull a wall | lintel |
| the climb ignored | stairs up, stairs to a landing, half slabs up |

Nothing the footprint could do failed stairs down: it walls only a rise. The descent test stays
as a guard.

## The footprint allocates nothing

`VoxelShape.bounds()`, its column queries `min/max(Axis, a, b)` and `forAllBoxes` all allocate. A
`CubeVoxelShape` builds its coordinate lists on every call. So 1.10.0 allocated for every block
the walk read, and bisection runs the check about 21 times a move. `ShapeBoxes` unpacks a shape's
boxes once and keeps them by the shape's identity: a block's shape is one object per state, and
the garage door's is one per panel travel. The table is direct-mapped, 1024 slots of immutable
entries, which the client and the integrated server share without a lock. The six points and the
block position are no longer allocated per call either. Client and server run the same code on
the same shapes, so they still agree (D-0007).

## The terrain pose: checked, and a second fault found

`Vehicle.columns()`, which the drawn pose reads (`domain/Terrain`), reads a block column whole:
a shape's top and bottom, never the point within the cell. That is Terrain's model. The ground
is constant per block column, and a wheel's disc meets it at block edges. So a door's edge cell
reads as a column of track to the door's top.

The fit's walks judged a wall face only against the sample before, and took each walk's first
sample unchecked. Where a walk began over a track column, the plane took it as ground three
blocks up. With 1.10.0's Terrain, through open doors on a level floor, the drawn body rose up to
1.24 blocks and pitched up to 6.1 degrees (the truck, 3x3) and 1.45 and 7.1 (the truck, 4x4).
The box car, through the 3x3 door this change's footprint opens to it, rose 2.09 and pitched 8.8.
It happens wherever a wheel track runs through a door's edge cell, so pack 1.69.0 does it to a
Trailblazer in any 3- or 4-wide door.

Fixed: a walk starts from the ground the body stands on, its own y, and a first sample more than
the climb (plus 0.2) over that is a wall face like any other. The box's y is the top of the
ground under it, which includes the centre. So this changes the pose only where a walk begins
over a column the box does not stand on:

- a door's track column under a wheel track;
- a wheel track that sits outside the box (the Trailblazer's wheels are 0.13 outside it) inside
  a wall the box runs alongside, where the body rolled toward the wall;
- a trailer axle outside its box, over a rise of more than the climb.

In each case the body now keeps to the samples it can reach instead of lifting toward the
column. The second case, measured before the release in
`TerrainTest.alongAWallItsWheelOverhangsTheBodyStaysLevelButACurbStillTiltsIt`: beside a wall three
high, 1.10.0's fit rolled the Trailblazer's shape 35 degrees toward the wall and 1.10.1's keeps it
level; a curb one high, within the climb, still tilts it. `TerrainTest.throughAnOpenDoorwayWhoseEdgeColumnsAreItsTracksTheBodyStaysLevel` lifted
1.24 before the fix and stays level after it, and every other terrain test is unchanged. The
drive-through gametest now asserts each vehicle on its wheels and level through all three doors;
on 1.10.0's Terrain it failed at 6.29 degrees. `columns()` itself is unchanged: reading columns at
the point would change every staircase's drawn angle.

## Rejected

- **Testing each box at the point, or the column under it.** They move stops at fences and walls,
  and the column read also walls the stair flight above. Measured above.
- **Anything door-local.** Reshaping the housing or the tracks leaves the union's bounds the same.
- **Removing the tracks from the door's collision.** It would fix both readers at this door, but it
  changes the door for players and mobs, and it leaves every other block whose boxes stand at
  different heights (a bed, a hopper, an anvil) read as its bounds.
- **Reading the pose's columns at the point.** Explained in the section above.

## Not fixed

- **A post between two footprint points.** The footprint samples six points and the ground along
  the walk to each. A post or arm narrower than the gap between two points can pass between them
  and end inside an overhang, as deep as half its width: 0.125 for a fence and 0.25 for a wall
  in the model. 1.10.0 and 1.10.1 behave the same here. Closing it means sampling the nose and
  tail edges densely, which costs the check per move.
- **A climb over the hull's height.** A profile whose climb is more than its hull is tall still
  meets a full block across its climb line as a wall, even when the hull would pass under it.
  The box car (climb 2.0, hull 1.6) is one. No shipped vehicle is.
- **Trailblazer's `TrailblazerPlaytest`** was not run. It is a client run that needs a display, and
  it resolves Vanilla Wheels 1.8.0 from mavenLocal.
