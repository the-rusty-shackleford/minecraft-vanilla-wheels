# D-0030 — A protocol may be layered on this one: vehicle kinds, overridable hooks, wheels not drawn

Rusty asked for a Huey and a Chinook (2026-10-06), then for Rotorcraft to be "its own analog to
the vanilla wheels protocol". Flight shares little with driving: its own physics, controls, safety
rules (nobody aboard is ever hurt by a fall; crashes wear the aircraft instead), a mixin so Shift
can mean "down", a sling and a crop sprayer. It shares everything else: seats, chests, cargo,
paint, the lift, keys and recall, fuel, condition and repair, towing links, the renderer. Before
1.12.0 the only way in was to edit `Vehicle`, a 2 700-line class, in a mod called Wheels.

**Decision**: another protocol may build vehicles of its own on this one.

- `api/VehicleKinds` is a registry of kinds. A kind claims profile ids (decided by what both sides
  see, such as the protocol's own synced registry) and names the entity type that makes them; its
  class extends `Vehicle`. `Vehicle.create` asks the kinds first, so the lift's Build, a vehicle
  item set down, a trailer put on a hitch and a key's recall all make the right type. Profiles no
  kind claims keep `vanillawheels:vehicle`. The headlamps register with Luminance for every kind's
  type.
- `Vehicle` opens the points such a protocol needs, each defaulting to exactly what it did before:
  `takeTheWheel`, `stepAtTheWheel` and `poseAtTheWheel` (a tick at the wheel); `follow` and
  `poseTowed` (how a towed body follows and poses); `footprintClamp`; `burnsFuel`, `runsOver`,
  `breaksFragile`; `engineRunning` and `engineLoad` (what the engine loop plays); and, for a
  subclass's own use, `columns`, `rideTheGround`, `sharePose`, `takeSharedPose`,
  `playerAtTheHead`, `showSpeed` and `pose`.
- `VehicleRenderer` can be extended: `appearance` names the parts the subclass moves itself, and
  `drawExtras` draws them in the body's frame. `Appearance.of(profile, extras)` cuts the extras out
  first (`Parts.cut`), so no face is drawn twice and a painted rotor blade is not also body paint.
  The `solid` buffer `drawExtras` is handed is live: wheels with a texture of their own ask the
  buffer source for another type, which in the level's immediate source ends the body's batch, so
  the body's is asked for again before the extras draw. (The Chinook, a wheel mesh of its own and
  two rotors, crashed every client with "Not building!" before this; its booth found it.)
- `Wheels.drawn` (default true). False makes the wheel positions only where the vehicle touches
  the ground: a helicopter's skids, a container's feet, drawn with the body. No wheel mesh is drawn
  there, and the lift builds the vehicle without wheels (`Assembly` allows none).
- `EngineSound` plays a sound id the registry does not know through a `SoundEvent` made for it, so
  a data-only vehicle mod can ship its own loop in its own `sounds.json`. The loop's note and
  loudness run between the profile's `sounds.pitch` and `sounds.volume` ([idle, full], absent a
  car's 0.75 to 1.6 and 0.28 to 0.72): a car's engine climbs a long way, but a rotor turns at one
  speed, and played at a car's top note a Huey's 94 ms blade beat becomes a toy's 60.
- A profile may name its third-person camera's distance (`camera`, blocks behind a rider's eye);
  absent, a length's worth as before (1.5 and 1.5 a block). The length rule put the camera 20 blocks
  behind a Huey's pilot, who sits in its nose: the helicopter was a speck in the middle of the screen
  (the booth's third-person shots, 2026-10-07).

This is the one exception to D-0001's single entity type: a vehicle *mod* still registers nothing;
a *protocol* layered here registers its entity types. None of this adds a payload or a synced
value, so the network version stays "6".

**Why**: the subclass reuses every system above as it is, and each extension point is one the
first layered protocol (Rotorcraft) needs for something specific. The policy hooks (`runsOver`,
`breaksFragile`, `burnsFuel`) are the server duties that differ for an aircraft; `follow` lets a
slung load hang from a rope instead of a drawbar. Keeping one entity type and composing behaviour
from providers would make this protocol design an interface for motion, falls, wear, footprint,
rendering, sound and towing all at once, with one user to test it against.

**Alternatives rejected**: *Flight inside Vanilla Wheels* (the first plan): the protocol for wheels
would own a flight model, a mixin and a sling it never uses itself. *A motion-provider registry on
the one entity type*: above. *A separate entity class copying `Vehicle`*: a second copy of seats,
chests, cargo, keys, recall and repair.

**Verification** (gametests in the tests' own mod, with a kind of its own, `SkidVehicle`, claiming
the box skids, the box car with its wheels not drawn):
`KindGameTests.aClaimedProfileIsMadeByItsKindsEntityAndAnUnclaimedOneByTheProtocols` (created,
and set down from its item), `aKindDrivesThroughItsOwnHookAndRunsNothingOverWhenItsPolicySaysSo`,
`aProfileSetsItsEngineLoopsNoteAndLoudnessAndOneThatDoesNotKeepsACars`,
`aProfileNamesItsThirdPersonCameraDistanceAndOneThatDoesNotGetsALengthsWorth`,
and `LiftGameTests.buildMakesAProfileWhoseWheelsAreNotDrawnFromItsChassisAndEngineAloneAsTheKindThatClaimsIt`.
With `Vehicle.create` ignoring the kinds, all three failed; with `runOver` called regardless of
`runsOver`, the cow lost 3.2 health and the second failed. JUnit: `AssemblyTest` (no drawn wheels,
powered or not), `PartsTest` (an extra is cut first; two extras share no face). Every test from
before (91 gametests, 124 JUnit) is unchanged and green.
