# D-0031 — A body that moves in three dimensions: its hull, its crashes, its keys

nfx sent a brief for two submarines (2026-10-07). It proposed an Immersive Aircraft addon,
reading this protocol as a jar without source. Rusty: "it's probably an extension of vanilla
wheels. It's a new vehicle." A submarine is then a second protocol layered here (D-0030). It needs
four things Rotorcraft built for its aircraft (its D-0001):

- a hull of boxes that meets the world in every direction;
- crashes judged on the server from the moves the pilot's client reports;
- Up, Down and Get out on Space, Left Shift and R;
- the mixin that keeps Shift from letting a rider off.

Asked where they should live, Rusty chose here (2026-10-07: "Lift into Vanilla Wheels"), over a
second copy in the submarine protocol.

**Decision**:

- **The hull.** `domain/Hull` (boxes probed at points no more than 0.9 apart, and how far they
  reach) moved here from Rotorcraft unchanged.
  - `Vehicle.hullPoints()` (null by default) names a body's points; the boxes stay in the
    protocol's own profile, which knows when its body moves in three dimensions.
  - `hullClamp(delta)` cuts a move short in any direction so no point ends inside a block, letting
    through a move that starts inside one; it uses `hullBlocked` and `inBlock`.
  - A protocol calls a clamp from `footprintClamp`.
  - `hullClamp` cuts the move as a whole: an aircraft in the air.
  - `hullClampAxes` cuts the rise or fall first, then the move across from that height, as the
    game's own collision does: a submarine. A hull resting on the seabed, its planes down, slides
    along it; cut as a whole, the touch would stop all of it.
  - Rotorcraft keeps the whole-move clamp, so nothing changes in flight.
- **Crashes.** `domain/Crash` is Rotorcraft's `Wear` with its three speeds a protocol's own: safe,
  touchdown safe, wreck.
  - `Vehicle.crashes()` is null by default: a car's crash costs it speed, not condition.
  - With one, the server judges every move the controlling player's client reports against the
    one before it: what the move lost beyond `ownChange()` (what the body's own model can change
    in a tick) the world took. It is charged once an impact through `crashed(wear)`, which wears
    it and logs it.
  - A move longer than six blocks (a recall, a correction) is never judged.
- **Vertical controls.** `Vehicle.verticalControls()` is false by default; true gives a body Up
  (Space), Down (Left Shift) and Get out (R).
  - The keys live in their own context, live in any seat aboard such a body: `Keys.UP`, `DOWN`,
    `GET_OUT`, and `Keys.lift()`.
  - A mixin on `Player.wantsToStopRiding` keeps Shift from letting a rider off; one on
    `updatePlayerPose` keeps a rider holding it from being drawn crouching (the seat hangs from
    the eye).
  - Get out sends `Payloads.GetOut`, and the server asks `Vehicle.getOut(rider)`: out by default;
    an aircraft refuses high in the air.
  - `Keys.RidingKey` is public, so a protocol's own keys judge their modifier as the game's keys do
    (1.12.0's horn fix). Rotorcraft's G and V now do.
- **A fuel rate.** `Vehicle.fuelRate()` (1 by default) is the ticks of fuel burnt for each tick the
  engine burns. `domain/FuelDebt` carries the fraction from tick to tick; the tank keeps whole
  ticks. This is for a protocol's upgrades: the submarines take Immersive Aircraft's.
- **Placement.** `VehicleItem.place(level, player, stack, at, yaw)` is what a click on a block face
  always did, made public, so a protocol sets a vehicle down elsewhere (a submarine on open water)
  through the same recovery, room and use-up rules.
- **The cockpit's glass.** Glass that the `cockpit` selector names as well is a piece of its own
  (`Parts.cockpitGlass`): drawn as glass for everyone else, not drawn for a rider looking out
  through their own eyes. Before, a face in both was glass and always drawn, and a face in the
  cockpit alone is drawn solid. The submarines need it: a bubble or a port built of voxel boxes
  stacked its faces' edges across the view from inside (the Scout's booth photo, 2026-10-07). No
  vehicle before them names glass in its cockpit (every shipped and gametest profile checked), so
  nothing else changes.

The network version goes from "6" to "7" (the get-out payload). Nothing changes for a car.

**Why**: D-0030 kept one protocol's needs out of this one while it had a single user to test them
against. A second protocol needing all four is the reason to share them. Two copies would mean two
mixins on the same two methods, and the same three keys listed twice in Controls. The next fix to
the keys, like 1.12.0's (found only in Rotorcraft's playtest), would have to be made twice.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *A copy in the submarine protocol*: Rusty's call, above.
- *Hull boxes in this protocol's profile*: only the protocol knows when its body moves in three
  dimensions (an aircraft on the ground drives as a car), so it keeps the boxes and hands over the
  points.
- *`Wear`'s three speeds fixed here*: a submarine is slower than an aircraft and should wreck at a
  slower crash.

**Verification**:

- JUnit:
  - `PartsTest`: glass the cockpit names too is the cockpit's glass, apart from the glass and the
    cockpit; without a cockpit selector it is glass like any other;
  - `HullTest`, moved;
  - `CrashTest`: `Wear`'s partitions with the speeds a protocol's, a slower body, refused speeds;
  - `FuelDebtTest`: a tick a tick for 1000 ticks with nothing owed; nothing; a quarter (a tick
    every fourth); Industrial Gears' 0.8; two Eco Engines' sixteenth; 1.3 and 1.8; the most; out of
    bounds.
- `ThreeDimensionGameTests` (6), each beside a control with the facility off:
  - Shift keeps a rider aboard and the get-out key lets them out, where a car's driver is dropped;
  - a hull stops the body short of a stone ahead, and of one over its nose, that its square box
    never touches;
  - met axis by axis, a hull pressed down into its floor slides a whole block along it, where met
    as one move it stops;
  - a reported stop is a crash charged once, a teleport never, a car never;
  - an engine at a quarter of the rate burns a quarter of the fuel.
- Each was run against its mutation, and each break was caught by the expected tests:

  | Mutation | Tests failed |
  |---|---|
  | the mixin's return removed | 1 |
  | no hull clamp | 2 |
  | no crash charged | 1 |
  | the rate ignored | 1 ("40 of 40") |

- Everything from before is unchanged and green: 146 JUnit, 103 gametests, the booth's 54 checks.
  The booth's first run failed one check, "H with Left Control held cycles the lights" (lights OFF,
  before OFF), with the key read down. Its code path is untouched, and the re-run passed. Most
  likely a timing flake (the key helper's start eating the window); pin it if it comes back.
  (It came back, and it was not timing: D-0032.)
- The cockpit's glass: `PartsTest`'s new test, which fails with the split taken out; the Explorer's
  and the Scout's booths see water through their ports and bubble from every seat.
- Rotorcraft 1.1.0 moved onto this with no change in behaviour: 37 JUnit (its 49, less the 12 that
  moved), its 33 gametests, and the real-key booth's 13 checks (Space climbs, Down under a real
  Left Shift, G under Shift, a Shift let go off the aircraft not still down). The Huey's and the
  Chinook's 6 gametests each pass on it.
