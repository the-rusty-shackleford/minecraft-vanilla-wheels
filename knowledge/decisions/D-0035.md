# D-0035 — The Vehicle Sensor

Rusty, 2026-10-11, building an underground garage under his house with a hidden tunnel for vehicles
that "only opens and reveals itself when you are driving in or out ... think Batman": "Maybe using
vehicle pressure plates", and the sensor opens for "any vehicle probably". A pressure plate does not
see a vehicle (its hit boxes are not living, and a plate under a road cannot be pressed), and a
tripwire row across a road had to be crossed to fire. The first build was a tripwire: each vehicle
told the sensors under its body, buried up to two blocks deep, every half second. In his lab test it
was "too slow", and "the sensor also does not trigger when you are close to the door, I have to back
up a little bit for it to see me"; then "I think the sensor should be range based and not 'tripwire'
based".

**Decision**:
- The **Vehicle Sensor** is a block that gives full redstone power on every side, strongly (so it
  powers the block it touches and that block's neighbour, a door's sill and the door on it), while a
  vehicle is within its range: any part of one within that many blocks of the sensor, in every
  direction (`AABB(sensor).inflate(range)`).
- A vehicle is any of Vanilla Wheels' (cars, trucks, trailers, helicopters, submarines, any part of
  one's body), or anything not alive that carries a player (a boat, a minecart, another mod's aircraft).
  A player on foot, a mob, a ridden horse or an item is not.
- It looks every 2 ticks (`Sensing.SCAN_PERIOD`) and holds 5 looks after the last one that saw a
  vehicle (`HOLD`): half a second, enough to hold through a vehicle bobbing at the range's edge.
- The range is a block state (`range`, 1..32, default 8), stepped by clicking with an empty hand on a
  ladder of 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32 (sneaking down), shown over the hotbar. A click with anything in
  hand is the item's, so a road can be laid against a buried sensor.
- Recipe: steel, an observer and redstone, two sensors (`SOS / SRS / SSS`).

**Why**: a range reaches a car at full speed (18 blocks a second) a second before it arrives, from
either side, and a car standing at the door; a buried sensor beside the door does it with nothing
across the road. Looking from the sensor costs a short entity query per sensor every 2 ticks, and
nothing per vehicle.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *The tripwire* (built first): the vehicle told the sensors under its body. It needed rows across the
  road on both sides and right at the door, and still missed a car stopped between rows.
- *A pressure plate that vehicles press*: a plate is pressed by entities standing on it, at the
  surface; it cannot be buried, and a vehicle's hit boxes ride above it.
- *A sphere*: a cube is what the game's entity queries take, and what "within 20 blocks" means to a
  builder counting blocks.

**Verification**: `SensingTest` (the ladder up, down, round both ends and off it; the countdown) and
`VehicleSensorGameTests` (a car's hit box a quarter block inside the range and a quarter outside, a
range set to 4; a cow, a ridden horse, a player on foot, an item and an empty boat do not count, a
player in a boat and a car do; on beside a parked car and off half a second after it goes; a sensor set
on with nothing near lets go; clicks step the ladder and a block in hand is laid; a sensor under a
Hidden Door's sill opens it for a car five blocks off). Each rule was run against its mutation and
failed (any non-living entity counting, a range one block too big, a block in hand stepping the range).
In Rusty's lab: a parked Trailblazer 18 blocks out opens the tunnel door in 0.41 s, one 23 blocks out
does not, and the door shuts 0.86 s after it goes.
