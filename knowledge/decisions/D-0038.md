# D-0038 — Seats kept, and a crew across a logout

OtatopMalloy died on the box on 2026-10-11 at 23:24:42 UTC. He was flying a Huey with Bobandy_ aboard.
The server log has both of their connections stalling from 23:24:04 and timing out a quarter of a second
apart (23:24:25.156 and .406). Jdrum12, on the LAN, stayed connected, and the server reported no lag.
What followed was the game's rule and ours:

1. The game keeps a vehicle in a player's save only while they are its one player aboard:
   `PlayerList.remove` and `ServerPlayer.addAdditionalSaveData` both ask
   `Entity.hasExactlyOnePlayerPassenger()`. With Bobandy_ still aboard, OtatopMalloy was saved without
   it, standing where his seat was (a passenger is saved at the vehicle's middle at their own height):
   y 122.7, about fifty blocks over the ground.
2. Seats went by boarding order, so with the pilot gone everyone moved up a seat, and Bobandy_ was
   handed the controls.
3. Bobandy_ timed out as the Huey's one player and took it into his save.
4. OtatopMalloy logged back in at 23:24:39 to nothing under him, and fell. Bobandy_ logged back in
   at 23:24:44 at the same coordinates, and the Huey came back with him.

Before his last flight that night, his 22:30 login brought his Huey back with him: alone, the game's
rule works.

Rusty: "I think we need to rethink the idea of when the pilot/driver exits, someone else becomes
driver. We need to rethink it in such a way that bailing out like that doesn't get you killed later."
Given seats kept and a take-the-controls key: "take the controls key sounds good". Asked whether a
returning rider goes back into the vehicle wherever it is or only near where they left, he did not
say; the recommendation (wherever it is, while it is loaded) stands.

**Decision**:

- **A rider keeps their seat** (`domain/Seats`, synced and saved with the vehicle as text).
  - Boarding takes the driver's seat while it is free, else the first free seat, as before.
  - Leaving frees only the leaver's seat. Nobody moves up, so the driver's seat is never handed to
    whoever boarded next.
  - With nobody in the driver's seat, nobody controls the vehicle, and the server steps it as it
    always has with nobody at the wheel: a car coasts to a stop, an aircraft settles to the ground
    unpowered with nobody hurt (Rotorcraft's D-0001), and a submarine holds its depth while it has fuel.
  - The players aboard are told once, over the hotbar: "Nobody is at the controls. Press U to take
    them".
- **Take the controls** (`Keys.TAKE_CONTROLS`, **U**, *Take the controls* under Vanilla Wheels in
  Controls):
  - It is live in any seat but the driver's, and moves the rider into a free driver's seat
    (`Payloads.TakeControls`, `Vehicle.takeControls`).
  - With someone at the controls it says who, and nothing moves.
  - U is one of the two letters (I and U) that nothing in Rusty's instance binds.
- **A crew across a logout** (`Crew`):
  - Every player aboard keeps, in their own save (the game's per-entity data,
    `vanillawheels:aboard`), which vehicle and seat they are in, and drops it when they get out. It
    is rewritten whenever the seats change, so an autosave carries it too.
  - The game's rule for a lone rider is untouched: the vehicle goes with them and comes back with
    them.
  - Logging in not aboard with a vehicle kept, they are put back in that vehicle wherever it is now,
    if it is loaded in their dimension and has a seat: their own while it is free, else boarding's.
  - Otherwise they are stood somewhere safe (`domain/Landing`):
    - in open air, on the first floor under them, a block's top or water's surface;
    - in water, at the surface over them;
    - only where they fit, never onto lava, fire, a magma block, a cactus, a berry bush, powder snow
      or a wither rose;
    - their own column first (their own x and z where they fit), then outward ring by ring, up to 8
      blocks.
  - Nowhere within reach, they get a minute of slow falling and water breathing to find their own
    way.
- The network version goes from "7" to "8" (the take-the-controls payload).

**Why**:
- The seat rule did not kill him by itself. With no handover at all, Bobandy_ would still have been
  the last one aboard and taken the Huey along.
- What kills is logging back in where a seat was, with the vehicle gone or gone elsewhere. That is
  answered at login, whatever the seat rule is.
- The handover was still wrong, as Rusty said. It moved a passenger into the controls without their
  asking. A passenger whose client was itself stalling, as Bobandy_'s was, then "flew" an aircraft
  the server would not move: it waits for the controlling player's client. Nobody at the controls is
  safer: the server brings the aircraft down itself.
- Kept seats also give a returning rider a seat to come back to.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *The vehicle never goes into anyone's save* (override `hasExactlyOnePlayerPassenger`): one rule
  for one rider or many, but a lone pilot's aircraft would be left in the world unattended while they
  are away, where the game's rule keeps it safe with them. Lone riders were not the problem.
- *Slow falling for anyone logging in high*: it does nothing for a submarine's crew under water, and
  drifts them wherever. Standing them on the ground under where they left is exact.
- *Put a returning rider back only near where they left*: a vehicle still loaded is one their crew
  is still in, wherever it flew; reseating them there is what "aboard" means.

**Not fixed here**: the server's "moved wrongly" resets of reported aircraft moves, seen on the
Huey and on Immersive Aircraft's planes alike. Their cause is unpinned, with one candidate (the
server re-runs a move at the previous tick's yaw, and the hull turns with it). Filed as issue #2.
The "moved too quickly" floods are packets bunched by a stalled connection: a symptom of the
timeouts, not their cause.

**Verification**:
- JUnit (180, 11 new):
  - `SeatsTest` (7): boarding, a preferred seat, leaving (nobody moves up), taking the controls,
    dropping absent riders, the text form with bad text.
  - `LandingTest` (4): open air over a block, a slab, a fence, water, a harm and nothing; standing
    on the floor or inside a block; no room for the head; water's surface, a ceiling over it, water
    to the top; bounds.
- `CrewGameTests` (6), on real players logged out and in through the player list
  (`connection.disconnect`, then `placeNewPlayer` with the same profile, from their save):
  - whoever leaves frees only their own seat; nobody is handed the controls; the key takes them,
    and does nothing with someone there;
  - a rider who logs out with others aboard is seated again on logging in, in their own seat, with
    the vehicle now on the ground;
  - one who logs in to the vehicle gone (the last one aboard took it along) is stood on the floor
    straight down from where they were saved, and unhurt, and the crew comes back in it;
  - the same over a magma block: they are stood beside it;
  - the same under water: they come up at the surface;
  - a lone rider still takes the vehicle along and comes back in it, at the controls.
- On the old code, the five crew tests then written failed and the lone rider's passed:
  - the crew was handed the controls;
  - a returning rider was not seated;
  - the pilot logged in 5.8 blocks up;
  - beside the magma, 5.8 blocks up as well;
  - 4.2 blocks under the surface.

  | the old move-up on leaving | 2 (the seat test, and a returning rider's own seat) |

- The booth (60 checks, 5 new), on a real U press: behind a stand-in at the wheel, the booth's
  player is a passenger; the stand-in gone, nobody is moved into the driver's seat; the line over
  the hotbar names U; U takes the controls.
- Rotorcraft 1.2.1 on the box helicopter, 26 blocks up (its `CrewGameTests`, 2):
  - the pilot drops out, the crew keeps their seat and comes down in it unhurt, and the pilot logs

  - OtatopMalloy's night: the pilot is stood on the ground unhurt, and the crew comes back in the

  - On the Vanilla Wheels Rotorcraft nested before (1.14.0), both failed: "nobody is handed the

  1.16.0 for the check, not changed), pass on it.
- Not seen: any of it on the box, or a crew logging out of a real aircraft.
