# D-0039 — A reported move is re-run at its own heading

On the box on 2026-10-11 OtatopMalloy's Huey was put back by the game's vehicle move check ("Huey
(vehicle of OtatopMalloy) moved wrongly!") in bursts: 18 lines in `2026-10-11-2.log`, 11 in
`2026-10-11-3.log`. A burst's first line was about one tick of travel and the next ones its
multiples (0.62, 1.23, 1.87). So the server's re-run of the reported move moved the Huey almost
nowhere, while the client had moved a full tick and kept flying until the correction reached it.
Found in the investigation of his death that night (D-0038), first filed as issue #2. Rusty: it is
our repo, so fix it.

**The cause**:
- Every vehicle's tick turns, then moves: a car's (`stepAtTheWheel`), an aircraft's, a submarine's.
  The client then reports where the vehicle went and its new heading.
- `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.handleMoveVehicle` re-runs the move with
  `entity.move(MoverType.PLAYER, delta)` at the heading the vehicle had before. It takes the
  reported heading only afterwards (`absMoveTo(x, y, z, yRot, xRot)`), whatever the re-run said.
- Our footprint (a car's) and our hull (`hullBlocked`, an aircraft's or a submarine's) turn with the
  body. So the server tested them a tick's turn behind the client.
- A Huey's hull reaches metres from its middle. Turning near a tree, a wall or the ground, its tail
  could clear a block at the new heading and meet it at the old one. The whole-move hull clamp then
  cut the move to nothing, and the game put the Huey back.

**Decision**: a mixin on `handleMoveVehicle`, just before that `move`, turns one of our vehicles to
the reported heading (`Vehicle.faceReported`), so the re-run tests the footprint or hull where the
client did. The game sets the same heading right after in every outcome (taken, or put back, which
puts it back at its old place with the new heading), so nothing else changes. Other mods' vehicles
are left alone. Immersive Aircraft's planes get the same resets on the box (40 lines) by their own
code, and those remain.

**Why not something else**:
- D-0007 kept the game's vehicle packet and its validation rather than a move packet of our own, and
  this keeps both. It only puts the body where the client had it when it moved.
- *Taking the heading from one of our own payloads* in `move`: the payloads and the vehicle packet
  are not ordered against each other within a tick.
- *Letting a reported move skip the server's hull clamp*: that would trust the client to stay out of
  blocks, which the re-run exists to check.

**Not from the heading**: the "moved too quickly" floods. They are packets a stalled connection
delivers in a bunch: the game refuses a move of more than ten blocks from where the vehicle stood at
the start of the server tick, and leaves it there, so each later packet in the bunch is a tick
further away. OtatopMalloy and Bobandy_ were bunched in the same millisecond at 15:22:38 and
18:02:38, and timed out together at 23:24:25, while Jdrum12 on the LAN stayed connected. That is
their network path.

**Verification**:
- `ThreeDimensionGameTests.aTurningPilotsReportedMoveIsReRunAtTheHeadingItWasFlownAt` sends a real
  `ServerboundMoveVehiclePacket` through the pilot's own connection and the game's handler. The box
  skids hang in the air with a nose reaching four blocks ahead, beside a stone. The pilot's client
  turns 45 degrees and moves 0.6 along the new heading.
- The test checks its own setup:
  - the nose clears the stone at the start;
  - turned, the whole move is clear;
  - unturned, the move would end in the stone.
- Without the mixin, the game logs "Box Skids (vehicle of turning-pilot) moved wrongly! 0.6" and
  moves it (0, 0, 0): the box's burst shape. With it, the move and the heading are taken.
- An artifact found on the way: the game's re-run asks a millionth of a block lower than the report.
  A hull resting its bottom points on a floor reads that as into the floor and is cut. An aircraft
  uses the car rule on the ground and a submarine cuts the rise or fall first, so neither meets it,
  but the test vehicle had to hang in the air.
- Everything else stays green on it: Vanilla Wheels' gate, and Rotorcraft's, the Huey's and the
  Chinook's, whose booths fly through the same handler in singleplayer.
- Not seen: the box's log without the bursts. After a release, a turning Huey near trees should log
  no "moved wrongly"; any that remain have another cause.
