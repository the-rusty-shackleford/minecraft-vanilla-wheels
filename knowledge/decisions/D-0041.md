# D-0041 — The keyring

Rusty, 2026-10-13, before the 1.17.0 release: "if you have multiple keys bound to vehicles, things
get unwieldy quickly (gotta store multiple keys in inventory and shit). What if we added a new
keyring item that somehow holds infinitely many fobs or something such that its easy and storage
efficient to select which vehicle you want to use the fob on?" On the preview (a ring with an active
key, sneak-scroll to choose, a screen to add and take off): "I think all bound keys should
automatically go on the keyring and it should be an impossible-to-lose item (dropping it should be
impossible, it persists after death, etc). Instead of all this sneak clicking weird shit, I think it
would make sense if right clicking it brings up a little UI that lets you select options, shows the
fuel costs for recovery and all that jazz." He also asked that a crafting recipe be checked for
clashes; the ring needs none.

**Decision**:
- **One ring a player, made by itself.** `Keyring` (`vanillawheels:keyring`, a list of key fob
  stacks in `KEYRING_KEYS`, the owner in `KEY_OWNER`). Pairing a vehicle with a blank uses the blank
  up and puts the key on the player's ring, making the ring into their inventory if they have none
  (it lands in the first empty slot: the one the blank just left, so the first pairing puts the
  ring in your hand). A key re-issued by a blank in the air goes on the ring; a key that comes home goes on
  the ring; a ring that comes home has its keys absorbed into the player's ring; a loose live key
  from before 1.17.0 is gathered onto the ring at login, at respawn, when a key comes home, and
  when the loose key is used. A retired key (a vehicle re-keyed) leaves the ring. No recipe.
- **Never lost, as a whole** (`KeyFobs`, generalised: a ring carrying a live key is live, with the
  ring's owner). The drop key does nothing to a live stack (a mixin on `Player.drop(boolean)`, on
  both sides so the slot does not flicker); a throw out of a screen is refused (`ItemTossEvent`),
  and the stack is looked for on the player at the tick's end and sent home if the game took it
  out anyway; a chest, a stranger's inventory, a frame, a pot, a bag or box then dropped: as a key.
  (1.17.1 flagged the ring as fitting in no container item, so bags refused it; Backpacks+ read that
  flag as "this is storage". 1.17.2 drops the flag, with Backpacks+ 0.8.0: Rusty wants a bag to take
  what the inventory takes, and the never-lost rules already cover a ring in a bag.) At a death the ring is
  lifted off before anything drops and comes back at the respawn. The waiting list is a list of
  stacks, keys and rings alike.
- **Use opens the ring** (`KeyringMenu`, no slots; `KeyringScreen`, drawn by hand). A row per
  pairing: the paint, the name, where the vehicle is as the index knows it (`domain/Whereabouts`:
  deployed, packed on the ground, or collected; the live position when loaded in the player's
  level; about how far, to two figures; whether in the player's dimension) and the share of a
  tank a recall costs from there (`RecallCost.share`: the quote's own curve). **Recall** on a row
  starts the request (`RecoveryData.beginFromRing`): the chunk ticket, the exact quote into the
  row with Confirm and Cancel; **Confirm** (`confirm`) lets it go through on the next tick, at
  the quoted price; a changed quote wants a fresh Confirm; Cancel, the screen closing, the key
  leaving the player, or the player dying ends it unpaid. The rows, the request and the last
  word said (`notices`) come down in one payload, `KeyringRows`, whenever they change; the
  buttons are the menu's own button clicks. Network 9. The hold-to-recall of a loose key is gone.
- **The ring at a vehicle** refreshes the mark of its own key; at a vehicle it has no key to it
  says so (`ring_no_key`); it pairs nothing. Packing already asks the pairing's owner, not the hand.

**Why automatic and whole**: a key that must be put on the ring by hand is a key that is not on it;
a ring that can be dropped is a bag. Rusty asked for both outright.

**Why a screen, not gestures**: he rejected the sneak-scroll and sneak-use shapes; the screen also
has room for what a fob in the hand could not say -- where every vehicle is and what each costs --
before anything is pressed.

**Rejected**: *a crafting recipe* (no need; nothing to clash with). *An active key* on the ring: with
a screen that recalls per row, nothing else reads a chosen key. *Taking a key off the ring*: a loose
key is only a way to lose one; a stranger cannot be handed one in any case.

**Verification**: `RecoveryTest` (the share curve, the whereabouts' rounding and recallability),
`KeyringGameTests` (pairing makes the ring and puts each marked key on it, the ring at a vehicle
refreshes its own key and pairs nothing, a stranger's blank makes no ring, the ring recalls a
vehicle through Recall and Confirm; a re-keyed vehicle's old key leaves the ring and is an ordinary
item; the drop key does nothing and a throw, a chest, a stranger, a frame and a dropped bag give the
ring back, one ring; a death keeps every key and a loose key joins the ring at login; a blank in the
air is refused with every key on the ring and re-issues one after a clear; the rows say where each
vehicle is, a recall waits for Confirm, Cancel charges nothing, Confirm packs the vehicle and the row
says collected), `RecoveryGameTests` (recall economics through the ring's Recall and Confirm, the
collected wreck refused, the replacement after a clear). The booth photographs the ring's screen with
three vehicles and the ring in hand.

**Mutations** (each applied alone, the gametests run, the source restored), all caught by their own
tests: the drop key dropping a live ring; the blank not used up; a loose key never gathered; the
recall going through without Confirm; a retired key staying on the ring; a ring not counted live;
the ring at a vehicle it has no key to saying nothing (caught once the test asserted the refusal
and its notice, not only that nothing was paired).

**Found on the way**: the first ring lands in the slot the blank just left, so a test that puts the
next blank into the same hand overwrites the ring; the test helpers pair from the last hotbar slot.
A 3D element model for the ring drew as nothing in a slot and in the hand while loading without a
complaint; the icon is a flat 16-pixel texture drawn by `devtools/art/keyring.py`. NeoForge refuses
to send a custom payload down a connection that never negotiated the channel (a gametest's
player), so the menu sends the rows only where the channel is. Scheduling a per-tick task from
inside a scheduled gametest step crashes the server (the framework iterates its tick map); the
recall helper waits in a sequence instead.
