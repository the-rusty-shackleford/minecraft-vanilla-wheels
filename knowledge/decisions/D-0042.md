# D-0042 — The eight-long lift, and a lift keeps its shape

Rusty, 2026-10-14, the day after the first car parked on the box: "The farmers pickup doesnt really
fit on the mechanical lift, I think we should make it a little bigger so it fits such vehicles (and
then please fix the spacing that will undoubtedly get messed up in my garage as a result of this)".
The Pickup's body is 6.44 blocks long; D-0040's six-long deck put its tail a quarter block off the
wall and its nose a block and a third past the front edge.

**Decision**:
- **Eight long.** The deck grows from six cells to eight, five wide as before, the posts on the new
  corners and the wall across the new back row. The spot is still measured from the wall (`GAP`), so a
  body up to 7.06 blocks sits whole on the deck and the Pickup's nose ends 0.62 inside the front edge.
  Seven would not do: 6.06 from the wall's gap to the edge, shorter than the Pickup. `OVERHANG` stays
  a block and a half, so a long lift takes a body up to 8.56; nothing in the fleet is between.
- **A lift keeps the shape it was laid with.** `Footprint` is a value now, `SHORT` (six) or `LONG`
  (eight), and the controller and every part carry a `long` block state. Every lift placed since
  1.18.0 is `LONG`. A block saved before has no such state and reads the default, `false`: it is
  `SHORT`, its cells in the order they always were, and its indices, its spot, its wall and its
  drawing are exactly what they were. A part's index property runs to 46, the long shape's last.
- **Never regrown.** A short lift is not turned long on load the way the wall was grown (D-0040):
  the two new rows lie where anything may stand -- another lift's front, a wall, a car -- and
  there is no cell order that keeps a short lift's indices and adds two rows under its posts. A
  long lift where a short one stands is a break and a placement (one item back, one item used),
  and the README and the wiki say so. The six in Rusty's garage are re-laid by command from the
  generator that laid them (the server repo's `knowledge/operations/garage.md`), with the hall's
  north-east pillar moved a block off the new front post, the two docked vehicles moved aside for
  the swap and put back where they stood: a spot is measured from the wall, so they do not move.
- **Network 10.** A new block state changes the state table every block after the lift's parts
  sits in; a client on the old table would read the world wrong, and NeoForge checks registries,
  not states. The network version refuses an old client at the door.

**Why not shorten the gap or the overhang instead**: they are not the problem; the deck is shorter
than the vehicle. **Why not seven**: above. **Why the shape is in the blocks and not in the block
entity**: a part finds its controller from its own state with no level lookup (`LiftPartBlock.controller`);
the shape has to be there too, or a part of a short lift would look for its controller where a long
one's is.

**Verification**: `FootprintTest` (the two shapes by flag, each a deck, four posts and three wall
cells, the short one's order as 1.16.0 and 1.17.0 laid it index by index, the long one's over eight
rows, round trips, bounds, the wall's face, the bars and validity in each shape, the eighth row the
long one needs and the short one never asks for), `DockingTest` (what fits each deck, the fleet on
both, an eight-block body on the long one only; the spot two blocks deeper on the long deck, the
Pickup's nose past the short edge and 0.62 inside the long), and `LiftGameTests` (the item lays the
long lift facing each way with every block flagged; the item is refused for the eighth row's post
cell and a hole under the seventh row; a lift laid by command without the flag grows its wall in the
short shape and no deck past its sixth row; a six-long lift laid whole docks the car against its own
wall, two rows nearer than a long one would). The booth photographs the long lift.
