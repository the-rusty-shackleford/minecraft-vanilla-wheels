# D-0043 — The spot is the middle of the deck

Rusty, 2026-10-15, the first look at the re-laid garage under pack 1.88.0: "the trailblazer does not
center properly based on the new size of the lift (it is too far back)". D-0040 backed every vehicle up
to the wall, tail a quarter block off its face, because the Pickup was longer than the six-long deck and
centred would have stood inside the wall; D-0042 made the deck eight long and left that rule, so a
Trailblazer (4.64) sat with its nose 2.4 blocks from the front edge and its tail 0.25 from the wall.
Rusty's ask on D-0040 was "center the vehicle on the lift perfectly".

**Decision**: the spot's depth is the middle of the stretch from the deck's front edge to the wall's
face (`Footprint.wallFace() / 2`: 3.66 on a long lift, 2.66 on a short one) for any body that leaves at
least `GAP` (a quarter block) at both ends there (`Docking.centres`: length at most `wallFace - 2 GAP`,
6.81 long, 4.81 short): the nose is as far from the edge as the tail from the wall. A longer body backs
up to the wall as before, tail `GAP` off the face, nose over the front edge, up to `OVERHANG` past it.
The two rules agree at the boundary, so no body jumps between them. On the long lift the whole fleet
centres, the Pickup with 0.44 at each end; on a short lift the Pickup still backs up. A vehicle docked
under 1.18.0 is set on the new spot as it loads: snapped there if within `HOLD` (the Pickup, 0.19),
else let go and taken again through the rams (the Trailblazer, 1.09).

**Why the wall's face and not the deck's back edge**: the back row behind the bar is not parking
surface; what the eye reads as the deck is the plate from the stripe to the bar.

**Verification**: `DockingTest` (equal margins at least the gap for two bodies on each shape, the
boundary body under both rules, the backed-up branch by the longest body, the Trailblazer's and the
Pickup's numbers on each shape), `LiftGameTests` (the car spun on the east lift ends with the same
margin at nose and tail; the short lift's spot a block nearer than the long one's; the towing pair
re-stood across the middle where both spots lie now). The booth photographs the centred car.
