# D-0047 — Two wheels and a horse's jump

Rusty, 2026-10-23: add a dirt bike and a vintage motorcycle (two Blockbench models received that day)
to the roster; "they jump exactly as a ridden horse does, same controls". Named the Scrambler and the
Iron Horse, each a data-only repo on this protocol. Neither can be a car: a car's wheels are one wheel
mesh drawn at each position, its roll comes from the ground under four wheels, and its jump key drifts.

**Decision**:
1. **The jump is the horse's own.** `Vehicle` implements the game's `PlayerRideableJumping`;
   `canJump()` is true only for a profile with a `jump` block (`{"strength": s}`, absent the average
   horse's 0.7). The game then does everything a horse's rider sees: holding the jump key fills the
   jump bar in place of the experience bar, letting go calls `onPlayerJump(percent)` on the driver's
   client, which holds the wheel, and tells the server (`START_RIDING_JUMP`). The numbers are read
   from the 1.21.1 source (`AbstractHorse.onPlayerJump`, `executeRidersJump`; `LivingEntity.travel`)
   into `domain/Jump`: a bar of p percent jumps at 0.4 + 0.4 p / 90 of full strength, all of it from
   90; the launch is the strength times that share; in the air each tick's upward speed is the last
   one less gravity, times 0.98. A jump asked for in the air is made on landing, as a horse's is. An
   average horse at a full bar rises 3.12 blocks, a tap 0.62. Not taken from the horse: its forward
   lunge (a bike's speed is its drive's) and its rearing.
2. **The jump key never drifts** on a vehicle that jumps; on every other it drifts as before.
3. **The server keeps the jump** by taking the driver's moves as it takes every move; nothing new is
   needed there, and a test drives the whole arc through the game's own handler to prove it. A body
   in the air now meets walls from its own height (`footprintClamp`): the footprint walk had followed
   the ground down under an airborne body and judged walls from there, so a bike two blocks up was
   stopped by a two-block wall the game itself, which lifts a box before moving it across, would let
   it pass. This applies to every vehicle off the ground by more than its climb. And a body in the
   air, or in a jump until it lands, is drawn at its own height: the ground fit held the drawn height
   to the ground under it, and its probe reaches only two and a half blocks down, so the booth's photo
   of a bike at the top of a three-block jump showed it standing on the ground (the client knew it
   was 3.12 up). A car off a cliff was drawn sunk toward the ground it had not reached, too.
4. **Two wheels lean** (`lean`, `domain/Lean`): wheels in a line give the ground fit no roll, so the
   drawn roll is the bike's own, upright going straight, into a turn as far as balancing it needs
   (atan of speed times turn rate over gravity, held to the profile's most), and onto its stand,
   `parked` degrees, with nobody at the controls and the wheels still. The ground still gives pitch
   and height. Riders lean with the body, as they pitch with it.
5. **Moving parts of the body's own mesh** (`moving`, `domain/Linkage`): a part turns about an axis
   through a pivot, by `roll` (with the distance travelled, as a wheel of its `radius`), `steer` (with
   the front wheels' steer, about an axis up the steering head) or `ridden` (`degrees` while someone is
   at the controls: a stand folding up), and may be `within` an earlier part, so a front wheel rolls in
   the fork that steers it. Cut from the mesh as doors are, deepest first, each with its own lenses and
   paint. Roll and steer axes are normalised (a roll axis to the left, a steer axis up) so the sense of
   either never depends on which way the profile wrote it.
6. **On the lift a rolling part is a wheel.** A body whose wheel mesh is not drawn took no wheels to
   build (skids, feet); one whose own mesh rolls takes one wheel a rolling part, so a motorcycle takes
   two, as a car takes four (`VehicleProfile.wheelParts`).

**Alternatives**: a rearing, horse-like jump driven by the drift key (refused: Rusty asked for the
horse's controls, and the game already draws its bar for any `PlayerRideableJumping`); wheels as a
second wheel mesh (refused: the two models carry their wheels, fork and stand in their own groups, and
a front wheel must turn in a raked fork, not about a vertical axis); a bike balanced by the terrain fit
with a narrow track (refused: two points in a line fit any roll).

**Verification**:
- JUnit: `JumpTest` (the game's numbers as oracle), `LeanTest`, `LinkageTest`.
- GameTests (`BikeGameTests`, the `box_bike` profile): a full bar clears a two-block wall and lands
  beyond, a tap rises under a block and is stopped; on the code before the footprint change the full
  bar stopped dead at the wall's face two blocks up; with the pose not following it into the air,
  the drawn body was 3.06 blocks under the body. A jump asked for in the air is made on landing.
  Landing from a full jump, on the flat and off a three-block ledge, hurts no rider and no bike. A real
  player's client reports a full arc through `handleMoveVehicle`: every move taken, no kick, no harm.
  It leans right into a right turn and left into a left, and onto its stand parked. A car does not jump.
- The bikes' own booths press the real Space key (Rotorcraft's `xkey.py`) and photograph the jump bar
  and a bike in the air.
